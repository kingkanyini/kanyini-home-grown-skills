## GOLD STANDARD #1 — example-artist "My First Song" (free masterclass for aspiring musicians)

**Source session:** 2026-06-14. Audience: aspiring musicians, women/non-binary 25-45, the "rainbow sheep" who've been told they're "not talented enough" / "too much." Front-end to the Bad Bitch Experience incubator.

**Counsel:** Expert Counsel (Russell Brunson, Myron Golden, Marisa Murgatroyd).

### The final ad set (scored)

| Role | Line | Score | Formula |
|------|------|-------|---------|
| COLD hook | The reason your first song isn't written... isn't what you think. | 9.0 | Pattern 12 |
| WARM hook | Inspiring artist! What if your first song only took one hour to create? | 8.2 | Identity callout + curiosity |
| REVEAL | Inspiring artist — what if your first song was just one hour away? | 8.5 | Identity callout + Pattern 11 loop |

### Why these score high
- **Pattern 12 cold hook** redirects blame from a surface object ("talent") to an unseen mechanism — the belief-shift engine. Pure open-loop scroll-stop.
- All three are **soft-invite** (no hard CTA), make **zero income/fame/stream guarantees**, and ground in the client's own positioning.

## GOLD STANDARD #2 — Synthetic Example (illustrative, no real client)

A fully fictional set in a DIFFERENT niche, to show the formulas transfer and to give a copy-safe reference. Offer: a free "First Mile" workshop for people who want to start running but think they "aren't runners." (Made up — use for structure, not as client data.)

| Role | Line | Formula |
|------|------|---------|
| COLD hook | The reason you still haven't started running isn't what you think. | Pattern 12 |
| HOW-TO | How to run your first mile without ever being "a runner." | Pattern 10 |
| SECRET | The secret to your first 5K this season (it isn't willpower). | Pattern 11 |
| CALLOUT | Athlete. Still telling yourself you're "just not built for this"? | call-them-up + question |

Note the parallels to #1: Pattern 12 leads cold, the "without" clause names one objection ("being a runner"), the secret keeps an open loop ("it isn't willpower"), and the callout names them at the rung above ("Athlete," not "beginner"). Same machine, different domain.

## RULE: Call Them UP the Identity Ladder ("call them up" GOU)

When writing a callout, name the audience at the rung ABOVE where they are now, never the diminishing label they're trying to shed.

- **Origin:** "Aspiring artist!" scored 6.8 — "aspiring" reinforces the exact label the audience wants gone. Flipping ONE word to "Inspiring artist!" jumped it to 8.2.
- **Why it works (3 jobs in one move):** (1) pattern-interrupts the cliché the reader auto-skips, (2) elevates them to the identity they want, (3) delivers recognition before any pitch.
- **Guardrail:** the body must EARN the elevated label, or it reads as flattery.
- **How to apply:** find the niche's default diminishing label (aspiring, beginner, wannabe, "someone who sings a little") → replace with the rung above (inspiring, artist, star, leader) and/or pattern-interrupt the exact cliché phrase.

---

## RULE: Don't Repeat the Headline Phrase in the Body

If a phrase is doing the work in the headline, don't echo it in the body/primary text — the reader already saw it. Open the body with a fresh callout or a concrete sensory detail instead. Treat headline + primary text as ONE unit and audit for overlap before shipping.
