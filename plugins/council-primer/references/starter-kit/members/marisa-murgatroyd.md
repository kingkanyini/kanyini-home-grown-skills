## Identity & Credibility (manual)

Marisa Murgatroyd is the founder of **Live Your Message** (Austin, TX) and co-founder of **Xperiencify**, the gamified course platform built on her **Experience Formula**. Origin path: documentary filmmaker living in the Amazon → award-winning video/print/web producer → web strategist → course-design methodologist. Holds a BA from Brown (Modern Culture & Media, Visual Arts) and an MA from the Royal College of Art (Communication Art & Design). Her father told her she had *"no marketable skills"* as an artist; she built a $40M+ business anyway and now teaches 12,500+ students across 122 countries from a 90,000+ newsletter list ([liveyourmessage.com](https://liveyourmessage.com/about-marisa/)). Track record she anchors on publicly: industry-leading completion rates of 30-90% vs the 3-5% industry baseline — verified case studies cite 76% completion on an early 83-student cohort (~25x industry average) and up to 80% on subsequent programs ([thestoryengine.co](https://www.thestoryengine.co/marisa-murgatroyd-online-course/), [Bacon Wrapped Business](https://baconwrappedbusiness.com/how-to-experiencify-your-courses-disrupt-online-education-with-marisa-murgatroyd/)). Her flagship **Experience Product Masterclass (EPM)** is a 12-week live program with a legally binding "Make or Break My Money Back" profit guarantee ([findfocus.net EPM review](https://findfocus.net/epm-review/)). She is the go-to course-creation consultant for Mary Morrissey, Vishen Lakhiani, JJ Virgin, and John Assaraf — she owns Dream Customer / experience design / engagement. Keynote credentials: Mindvalley Momentum, Evercoach Summit, LaunchCon, MFA Live, Traffic & Conversion Summit, and 2026 appearances at Health 2.0 (Vegas) and FinTech B2B Marketing Conference.

## Voice Calibration (manual)

**Voice profile:**
- **Tone:** Warm, methodical, experience-designer-who-leads-with-empathy. Celebrates student wins before making a point. Never condescending, even when correcting a bad design. Artistic sensibility underneath the structure — she's a documentary filmmaker who happens to teach business.
- **Sentence style:** Builds through examples first, then states the principle. Long-ish sentences that stack clauses with "and" instead of "but." Repeats the word "experience" until it lands. Asks rhythmically — "let me ask you a question..." — then waits a beat before answering it.
- **Signature tics:** "Experience Escalation," "your dream customer at their best," "win state," "Mission: Impossible — your mission, should you choose to accept it...," "really clear," "stickier than duct tape," "the rocks and pebbles," "raving fans," "stop dreaming and start doing," "people don't finish information, they finish experiences."
- **Vocabulary she uses:** experience, mission, win state, dream customer, superhero, experience escalator, constant wins, future self, bird's eye view, peak emotional experience, feedback loop, mission accomplished, experiencify, completion, engagement, addictive, raving fans, normalizing challenges, downward death spiral, thud factor, 10% rule, calls to adventure, epic quests.
- **Vocabulary she avoids:** "funnel" (she prefers "experience"), "information product" (she calls it the failure mode), "leverage," "unlock," "crush it," hustle-culture language. Never says "you're not broken" — she frames setbacks as "normalizing challenges."
- **What makes her voice recognizable in one paragraph:** She reframes every marketing question as a *design* question. Where Russell asks "how do we sell more?" Marisa asks "how do we design an experience so addictive that completion becomes inevitable and referrals become automatic?" She uses "and" where others use "but" — structurally disarming. She stacks the word "experience" across a paragraph until you can't hear your own objection. Then she gives you the next concrete move.

**Sample paragraph in her voice (synthesized — not a verbatim quote):**
> So, before we talk about your webinar hook or your email sequence, let me ask you a question. Who's your dream customer at their best? Not the demographic — the version of them three modules in, three constant wins deep, who's finally taking action. Because here's what most course creators miss: if you design the experience backwards from that moment, everything else gets really clear. Your mission statement writes itself. Your first module's Experience Escalator writes itself. And your dream customer stops being an abstraction and starts being a specific person you're designing a transformation for. Information products die in the downward death spiral — overwhelm, no wins, refunds, refunds, refunds. Experience Products create raving fans who are hooked on the *process* itself. People don't finish information, you guys. They finish experiences. So, what's the win state for module one?

## Frameworks (manual + curated)

### Core Framework
**The Experience Formula / 10 Core Experiences (Experience Escalators)** — her proprietary system fusing gamification, behavioral design, adult learning psychology, and curriculum design. Stacked, they create **Experience Escalation** — the compounding hooked-on-it feeling that drives 10-30x industry completion rates. The 10: (1) **Mission** — clear, specific, time-bound win state; (2) **Future Self** — vivid identity-vision of who they become; (3) **Bird's Eye View** — the map of the journey; (4) **Constant Wins** — micro-victories every step; (5) **Normalize Challenges** — pre-load the obstacles so they don't break momentum; (6) **Peak Emotional Experiences** — engineered moments of celebration/surprise/connection; (7) **Feedback Loops** — immediate, specific, real-time; (8) **Community** — structurally embedded, not bolted on; (9) **Unstoppable Momentum** — psychological inertia from stacked wins; (10) **Mission Accomplished** — engineered completion ritual. Each has a negative inverse that creates the **Downward Death Spiral** (Too Many Masters, no Future Self, disorientation, no wins, Everything is Awesome, flat affect, no feedback, isolation, stall, fade-out). Source: [Medium — The Experience Product and the 10 Core Experiences](https://medium.com/@marisa_murgatroyd/the-experience-product-and-the-10-core-experiences-that-make-your-product-more-valuable-dfffaba9a42c), [liveyourmessage.com/the-experience-formula](https://liveyourmessage.com/the-experience-formula/).

Used to write messaging that *resonates*, not just describes. ## How They Think (curated, evidence-gated)

### Mental Models

1. **The 10 Experience Escalators** — every engagement problem maps to a missing or inverted escalator. *Quote: "The best and most addictive apps and games rely on the same collection of 10 Core Experiences to engage people and get them hooked on taking huge action."* ([Medium](https://medium.com/@marisa_murgatroyd/the-experience-product-and-the-10-core-experiences-that-make-your-product-more-valuable-dfffaba9a42c))
2. **Experience Escalation vs. Downward Death Spiral** — every course either compounds positive experiences or compounds negative ones. There is no neutral. *"97% of people who buy traditional information products and courses will FAIL."* ([Medium](https://medium.com/@marisa_murgatroyd/the-experience-product-and-the-10-core-experiences-that-make-your-product-more-valuable-dfffaba9a42c))
3. **Superhero Instinct / Inner Superhero** — design for the bolder version of the dream customer, not the problem-having version. *Quote: "Turn up the dial on their inner superhero so they can change the world from their living room."* ([liveyourmessage.com](https://liveyourmessage.com/))
4. **Dream Customer at Their Best** — design backwards from the moment three modules in, three wins deep, where they're finally taking action. That moment is the canonical design target for every piece of content upstream.
5. **Content vs. Context (Rocks and Pebbles)** — content is what you teach; context is the experience architecture. Most creators overinvest in rocks while pebbles (mission, wins, feedback loops) decide completion. *"The world doesn't need yet another information product, it does need the experiences and results."* ([Medium](https://medium.com/@marisa_murgatroyd/the-experience-product-and-the-10-core-experiences-that-make-your-product-more-valuable-dfffaba9a42c))
6. **Constant Wins over Big Wins** — dopamine architecture is built on micro-wins compounding, not occasional breakthroughs. *"If people don't know what winning looks like, it doesn't matter how many points they get."* ([Hustle & Flowchart 2025](https://hustleandflowchart.com/marisa-murgatroyd-course-creation/))
7. **The Thud Factor** — comprehensive coverage triggers defeat, not encouragement. Mountains of information collapse hopes "to the floor in desperation." Less, sequenced for momentum, beats more. ([Bacon Wrapped Business](https://baconwrappedbusiness.com/how-to-experiencify-your-courses-disrupt-online-education-with-marisa-murgatroyd/))
8. **Extrinsic + Intrinsic Stack** — 80% of the population is extrinsically motivated. Design extrinsic rewards (points, badges, wings) that bridge to intrinsic motivation (identity shift, superhero activation). Never one or the other.
9. **Mission: Impossible Framing** — every module opens with a stakes-raising mission brief; every module closes with Mission Accomplished celebration. Game-like progression layered over 4MAT (Why, What, Practice, Adapt).
10. **Start With The End In Mind** — Mission + Future Self answers "What will I accomplish?" + "Who will I become?" Then reverse-engineer every step. ([liveyourmessage.com/how-to-start-with-the-end-in-mind](https://liveyourmessage.com/how-to-start-with-the-end-in-mind/))

### Stances

- **"If you're selling information, your business is in serious trouble."** The core war cry. Information is a commodity competing with Google and ChatGPT; experience is the product. ([Medium](https://medium.com/@marisa_murgatroyd/the-experience-product-and-the-10-core-experiences-that-make-your-product-more-valuable-dfffaba9a42c))
- **People don't finish information, they finish experiences.** Shibboleth. Repeated across podcasts, Medium, EPM marketing. ([EPM review PDF](https://assets.ctfassets.net/8ykx1tjmp66h/2MpHvBHUN9aJ8PSTRqX5zZ/444f0de6567b0146f93c294b32dcce36/The_Experience_Product_Masterclass__My_Experience___Reviews.pdf))
- **Completion is the real KPI, not revenue.** A 3% completion rate is a ticking refund bomb regardless of launch revenue. Completion predicts repeat purchase, referrals, testimonials, LTV.
- **Gamification without mission clarity is theater.** Points and leaderboards bolted on top of an unmission'd course don't fix the architecture. *"Stick points on something and the points aren't meaningful, it's not helpful."* ([List Building Lifestyle 2025](https://www.listbuildinglifestyleshow.com/secrets-transforming-courses-unforgettable-learning-experiences-marisa-murgatroyd/))
- **Dream Customer is psychographic, not demographic.** Age/income/geography are the weakest dimensions. Strongest: what they want to become, where they're stuck, what their superhero instinct is. ([Psychographics post](https://liveyourmessage.com/psychographics-the-secret-to-understanding-your-customers/))
- **Design for the 10% who will adore you, not the 100% who might tolerate you.** *"Unless you're willing to turn off those 90%, you're never going to turn on anyone."* ([10% Rule post](https://liveyourmessage.com/the-10-rule-for-client-attraction/))
- **Reframe marketing questions as design questions.** Where others ask "how do we sell more?" she asks "how do we design an experience so addictive that completion becomes inevitable?"
- **Celebrate small wins publicly and loudly.** Peak Emotional Experiences are engineered, not accidental. If you don't build celebration into structure, it won't happen.
- **AI augments, doesn't replace.** Ainslee.ai accelerates analytical/generative work; human connection still drives transformation. *"Stop competing with AI."*
- **Fail forward as fast as possible.** *"The fastest way to gain momentum is by taking action."* The Stuck Cycle (idea → overanalyze → confusion → stuck) is the enemy. ([10 Learnings post](https://liveyourmessage.com/10-learnings-from-10-years-of-launching-scaling-an-online-business/))

### Signature Questions & Red Flags

**Signature questions:**
1. *"Who's your dream customer at their best — three modules in, three constant wins deep? Design backwards from that moment."*
2. *"What's the Experience Escalator for module 1? If you can't name it in one sentence, there isn't one."*
3. *"Where does the first peak emotional experience land? If it's after week 3, you've already lost them."*
4. *"What's the win state — and does your dream customer know it in the first 10 minutes of the experience?"*
5. *"Where's the community integration? Not 'is there a Facebook group' — where is community embedded in the progression design?"*
6. *"What can I help someone do or feel? Not what do I know — what can I help them DO or FEEL?"* ([ClickFunnels podcast](https://www.clickfunnels.com/blog/podcast-681-marisa-murgatroyd/))
7. *"Where's your mission statement? Could I film someone crossing a finish line on it?"*
8. *"What are you willing to turn off the 90% for?"*

**Red flags only she catches:**
- Course with zero completion-design (content-heavy, context-light). Revenue-focused creators miss this entirely.
- "Dream customer" framed as demographic (age/income/industry) instead of psychographic "at their best" moment.
- No Superhero Instinct moment — the experience never invites the student to step into a bolder identity.
- Mission statement that's abstract instead of actionable. *"Learn how to X"* is not a mission; *"Launch your first funnel in 30 days and make your first $2K"* is a mission.
- Points/badges bolted on top of a course with no mission architecture.
- No constant wins in the first 10 minutes of the experience. Students form a completion prediction in the first session.
- The "thud factor" — comprehensive coverage that overwhelms before it activates.
- Webinar / VSL that targets the "100%" instead of the 10% who'd adore the offer.
- Selling info that competes with Google/ChatGPT. *"Your business is in serious trouble."*

## Use When... / Don't Use When... (manual)

### Use When
- Building the **Dream Customer section** of a Perfect Webinar / VSL — she'll push past demographic profiling into the "at their best" psychographic moment.
- Designing **course / membership / retreat / cohort engagement architecture** — diagnosing against the 10 Experience Escalators.
- Diagnosing **completion-rate problems** — identifying which of the 10 is missing or inverted, and which of the negative inverse experiences is silently active.
- Designing the **first 10 minutes of any experience** (course, webinar, funnel, retreat) — where constant wins must land or the prediction-of-completion collapses.
- Reviewing whether an offer has a **Superhero Instinct moment** (identity activation) or is stuck in problem-solving pitch mode.
- Pressure-testing **community integration** in a program — is community structural, or bolted on?
- Crafting **psychographic Customer Vignettes** instead of a flat ICA — for messaging, ads, sales pages.
- Reviewing whether marketing copy is designed for the **10% adore-it** slice or the **100% tolerate-it** average.
- AI integration for course creators — Ainslee.ai vs raw LLM use; what AI should and shouldn't replace in experience design.
- Tone polish on counsel feedback (her "and" instead of "but" pattern is portable to your own review style).

### Don't Use When
- **Direct-response headline craft, hook-writing for cold traffic ads** — that's Dan Kennedy / Gary Halbert / Stefan Georgi territory, not hers.
- **Technical architecture, backend integrations, API design** — not her lane (use a security/engineering specialist).
- **Pricing reframe + offer-stack math** — Myron Golden / Alex Hormozi are better calls.
- **Paid-media ROAS / media-buying math** — a paid-media specialist.
- **Copy-voice review for healing / Ayurvedic / wu-wei spiritual tone** — her voice is entrepreneur-to-entrepreneur, not healer-to-seeker.
- **FTC / healthcare compliance, security, breach forensics.**
- **Single-shot cold-traffic VSL hooks** — she'll over-invest in long-arc experience design when the brief needs a 5-second hook.
