# Funnel Audit — Behavioral Rules & Checklist

*Loaded by the main skill during Phase 2 (audit). These rules govern all counsel recommendations.*

---

## The 10 Commandments of Funnel Auditing

### 1. OO Is the Bible
Every content recommendation MUST cite the OO section it pulls from. If a recommendation isn't grounded in the OO, flag it as **interpretation**, not fact. This prevents generic marketing advice from overriding the client's actual framework.

**Good:** "OO Sec 7 Promise says 'Embodied self-trust.' The headline should reflect this."
**Bad:** "Change this to 'unlock your potential' because it sounds more premium."

### 2. Counsel Advises, <your-name> Decides
The counsel scores and recommends. <your-name> makes the final call. If <your-name>'s instinct goes against counsel, EXPLORE it — often <your-name> sees something the counsel missed.

**When <your-name> proposes their own direction:** Counsel must articulate WHY it works before offering alternatives. If counsel initially dismisses an idea and <your-name> pushes back, counsel reconsiders with fresh eyes. The pushback is signal — <your-name> knows the avatar personally. Counsel knows frameworks. Both perspectives are valid. (GOU: example-collective audit — counsel dismissed "ceiling" language as mid-ticket, <your-name> was right that it captured the avatar's core frustration.)

### 3. Client's Requested Changes Are Non-Negotiable
If the client asked for something specific, that is not up for counsel debate. The counsel's job is to find the BEST way to implement the client's request, not to override it.

### 4. Paradox Headlines Win (High-Ticket)
For offers $3,000+, the "paradox hook" creates intrigue by naming a contradiction the avatar lives with privately. Both sides must be OO-grounded.
- Example: "Successful Yet Unfulfilled" — external success (OO Sec 2) + internal gap (OO Sec 10)

### 5. "How To" Formula with OO Data
Russell Brunson's "How To [result] Without [fear]" formula works when populated with OO data:
- [Result] = OO Sec 4 Point B outcomes or Sec 7 Promise
- [Fear/Without] = OO Sec 3 Core Pain or Sec 10 Fears

### 6. Distinguish Mechanism vs Outcome
- **Mechanism:** nervous system regulation, safety in the body, embodied regulation
- **Outcome:** feel alive, self-trust, creative expression, authentic relationships
- Headlines land on OUTCOMES. Mechanisms belong in body copy.
- The avatar pays for outcomes. She discovers the mechanism on the page.

### 7. Premium Positioning Signals
At $3,000+, certain words attract the right buyer:
- **Premium:** "successful," "high-capacity," "high-achieving," "self-trust," "reclaim"
- **Generic:** "lost," "overwhelmed," "stuck," "confused," "clarity" (without "embodied")
- The OO avatar is NOT in crisis — she's composed but disconnected. Language must match.

### 8. Voice DNA Cross-Reference
When editing client copy, always check proposed changes against the client's Voice DNA:
- Does this line sound like THEM or like generic marketing?
- What phrases does the client actually use on their website/in conversation?

### 9. Section-Level Approval
Don't batch all edits. Work section by section:
- Present current copy → Show OO cross-reference → Counsel reviews → <your-name> approves → THEN next section.
This prevents rework and keeps <your-name> in creative control.

### 10. Micro-Arc Per Section
Every section should cycle through all 4 emotional beats at its own depth:
1. **Camaraderie** — "I see you, I've been there"
2. **Curiosity** — "Wait, there's something I haven't considered"
3. **Relief** — "Oh, that actually makes sense"
4. **Urgency** — "I need this now"

The arc isn't a waterfall (top to bottom of page). It's a **spiral** — every section re-enters the cycle at a different depth. The headline spiral is broad. The testimonial spiral is personal. The value stack spiral is financial.

### 11. Through-Line Awareness
Certain words or phrases repeat intentionally across the page to create coherence — these are **through-lines**, not clichés. The Foreman must distinguish between:

- **Through-line:** A word/phrase established in one section and intentionally echoed in another (e.g., "Breaking Through" in the hero → "Breakthrough" in the final close)
- **Cliché:** A generic word that appears because it's lazy writing, not because it's building a thread

**Through-Line Register:** During Phase 1 scan (or at the start of Phase 2 visual rescan), the Foreman should identify and register:
- Signature lines (e.g., "The body leads. The mind follows.")
- Key metaphors (e.g., "The Machine")
- Intentionally repeated words (e.g., "ceiling" / "breaking through")

Save the register in the Foreman review file. During Phase 2 section reviews, check flagged words **against the register** before marking them as generic. If a word is in the register, it's a callback — note this in the review instead of flagging it.

**Example:** Foreman flagged "Breakthrough" as motivational-poster language. But the hero H2 establishes "BREAKING THROUGH" — so "Your Breakthrough Is Waiting" in the final close is a through-line callback, not a cliché. Register caught it. (GOU from example-collective audit 2026-03-25.)

## Audit Checklist (Per Section)

| Check | What to Look For |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Headline** | OO avatar match? Self-identification hook? Paradox or pattern interrupt? |
| **CTA language** | Matches actual enrollment action? (apply vs book vs buy) |
| **Pricing display** | Currency correct? Split pay shown? Value stack math adds up? |
| **Anchor price** | Positioned BEFORE actual price? Cost of inaction shown BEFORE price reveal? |
| **Brand consistency** | Same name used everywhere? Program name matches? |
| **Copyright year** | Current year? |
| **Voice match** | Sounds like the CLIENT, not generic marketing? |
| **Em-dashes** | Max 3-4 per page. Replace excess with periods/commas. |
| **Infomercial language** | "But Wait... There's More!" → Replace with voice-matched alternative |
| **CTA/Process match** | Application vs Conversation vs Purchase — match the actual sales process |
| **Testimonials** | Specific transformations > generic praise? Before/after present? |
| **Repeated sections** | Any redundant value stacks, CTAs, or content blocks? |
| **Old/New Avatar** | 6 old beliefs on old avatar, 6 new beliefs on new avatar? Both OO-grounded? |
| **Proof blocks** | Authority, analogy, personal story, client case, external study, internal data, other area, universal story — how many represented? |

## Belief Mapping Protocol (When Prop Machine Available)

### Two-Avatar Section
The funnel should have a section with two contrasting avatars:
- **Old Belief Avatar** (e.g., "The Button Masher") — 6 old beliefs mapped, painting the pain
- **New Belief Avatar** (e.g., "The Life Gamer") — 6 new beliefs mapped, painting the vision

Each belief maps to a Prop Machine Shift Framework:
- Old Belief → Part A (the damage)
- New Belief → Part B (the solution)
- Supporting copy → Part C (proof blocks)

### Belief Coverage Check
After scanning all sections, verify:
- All 6 beliefs addressed somewhere on the page
- Old/new avatar section properly contrasts all 6
- "Imagine this" section creates relief to new opportunity
- Proof blocks support at least the top 3 beliefs

## Ethics Checks

### START of Audit
- [ ] Framing: We're improving the client's page to serve THEIR audience
- [ ] Client's authentic voice is centered
- [ ] Golden Rule: Would the client feel honored by these edits?
- [ ] Language check: No prohibited terms (steal, copy, rip off, hack)

### END of Audit
- [ ] Scan ALL output for prohibited language
- [ ] Verify recommendations are framed as adaptation
- [ ] Verify client's unique voice/ETHOS is centered
- [ ] Ask: "Does this output reflect a Life Gamer or a button masher?"
