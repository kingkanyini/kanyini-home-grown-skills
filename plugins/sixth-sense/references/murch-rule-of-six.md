# The Rule of Six — cut priority

An original summary of the editing priority framework Walter Murch describes in
*In the Blink of an Eye: A Perspective on Film Editing* (Silman-James Press). Read the
book for the full argument; this file exists so the scanner has the priority order in
hand when it justifies a cut.

## The order

Murch ranks what a cut has to satisfy, and weights them. The weights are the point —
they are wildly uneven on purpose.

| Priority | Criterion | Weight | What it asks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Emotion** | 51% | Does the cut preserve what the audience should be feeling? |
| 2 | **Story** | 23% | Does it advance the story? |
| 3 | **Rhythm** | 10% | Does it land at a moment that feels right — not early, not late? |
| 4 | **Eye-trace** | 7% | Does it respect where the audience is already looking? |
| 5 | **Two-dimensional plane** | 5% | Does it hold screen direction and stage line? |
| 6 | **Three-dimensional continuity** | 4% | Do people and objects stay where they physically were? |

## Why it matters for scanning

The top item outweighs all five below it combined. That is the whole framework: when a
cut can't satisfy everything — and it usually can't — you sacrifice from the bottom up,
never the top down. A technically flawless cut that kills the emotion is a bad cut. A
cut that breaks spatial continuity but holds the feeling is usually the right one.

Applied here: when scoring or defending a segment boundary, lead with emotion and story.
Treat rhythm as the tiebreak. Only raise eye-trace, screen direction, or spatial
continuity when the higher criteria are already satisfied — or when one of them breaks so
badly it pulls the viewer out of the moment, which is really an emotion failure wearing a
continuity costume.

## The blink

Murch's related observation: people blink at moments of mental punctuation — the end of a
thought, a shift in attention. A cut placed where the viewer would naturally blink feels
invisible. A cut placed against that rhythm feels imposed. Useful as a sanity check on
rhythm, not as a measurement.
