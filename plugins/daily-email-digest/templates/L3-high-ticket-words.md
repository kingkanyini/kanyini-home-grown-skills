## Email 1: The "High Ticket Opener"

### Sales Psychology

*Notes: This is the first email that opens the conversation. The point of this email is to be short, direct and get people to raise their hands. You are filtering people who are interested and not interested. I do NOT recommend adding to this email. Keep it short, sweet and with a clear outcome. If you can not describe your entire offer outcome in 1 to 2 sentences then you need to make it more clear before proceeding.*

### Example by Joshua Sprague (2HB)
**Subject:** "writing centric" biz

I'm going to work with a small group of clients to help them build their own "writing centric" online business. The business would be set up to only need about 2-3 hours per day to run. Would you like to work with me?

Joshua

### Practice Questions

1. Can you describe your high-ticket offer outcome in 1-2 sentences? If not, what needs to get clearer before you write this email?
2. What is the single most desirable result your offer delivers — stated in plain, specific language your reader would use?
3. How will you signal exclusivity ("small group," "a few clients," etc.) without overexplaining?
4. What is the one question you will end with to force a yes/no hand-raise?
5. Look at your draft — is it under 5 sentences? If not, what can you cut?

## Email 2: The "Delayed Gratification"

### Sales Psychology

*Notes: The "Delayed Gratification" Method: Part of what makes the method of selling in this specific email so potent is that I don't ask for the sale right away. There are multiple points where I am pushing people away from the sale. I am essentially delaying their ability to buy.*

*This is counterintuitive to what most people experience during a sales process and it actually creates MORE desire to buy.*

*The first way I did this was after people said "YES" to wanting to work with me in the "High Ticket Opener" email, I then pushed them away by forcing them to commit to non-negotiables before they could even see the offer. The second way I did this in this email was by creating limited spots. This creates exclusivity in the offer which increases desire, as humans are more attracted to things that are scarce.*

### Example by Joshua Sprague (2HB)
**Subject:** Woah. That was crazy.

Okay.

That was crazy.

I sent an email yesterday asking if you wanted to work with me to build your own 2 hour a day "writing centric" online business.

Affirmative responses started flooding in *immediately*.

I've literally already gotten over 50+ responses saying, "Yes."

Full transparency, I do NOT have the bandwidth to work with 50 people in this new container right now while maintaining the quality and customer experience I want.

So, here's what I'm going to do.

You're seeing this because you were one of the people that said they wanted to work with me.

I'm only going to be able to work with maybe a dozen people right now to maintain the integrity of the deliverables (one of which is freaking nuts).

So, I'm going to trim the herd.

There are two non-negotiables if you are going to work with me in my new container The 2 Hour Business.

1. You MUST agree to write every day - no matter what. No days off. Period.

2. You MUST agree to walk at least 6000 steps per day. If you cannot prioritize moving your body, you cannot be in this container.

If you commit to BOTH of those things, reply to this email saying, "I'm committed to writing and walking."

After that, I will send you a Google Document that explains all of the details of this new container and how I can help you create your own writing-centric online business that takes around 2-3 hours a day to run.

Follow your higher calling,
Joshua "write and walk every single day" Sprague

Go ahead and read it and then come back here and reply to this email letting me know if you want one of the 12 spots.

### Practice Questions

1. What are your 1-2 non-negotiables that a prospect must commit to BEFORE they can even see your offer details? These should be values-aligned, not arbitrary.
2. How will you create genuine scarcity — limited spots, limited time, limited capacity? It must be real, not manufactured.
3. What specific keyword or phrase will you ask prospects to reply with? How does it reinforce their commitment?
4. Where in your email are you "pushing away" from the sale? Identify at least two points where you delay gratification.
5. Draft your reply template: what do you send after someone responds with the keyword?
