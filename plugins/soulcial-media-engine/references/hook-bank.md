## The Thumb-Stop Test

> **"Would the first line/frame stop YOUR thumb? If not, rewrite."**

This test gates every post at the P6 checkpoint AND in the Stage 2 Brock Johnson review. A thumb-stop failure raises `BLOCKER?: yes` on P6 — it is not advisory. Scroll context is brutal: the hook is not "nice to have." If it does not create an involuntary pause, the post does not get posted.

How to apply it: read the opening line or look at the cover frame as if you are scrolling at 1.5× on a crowded feed. Does it create a *pattern interrupt* — something that does not fit the expected content in that space? If the answer is "maybe" or "sort of," rewrite.

## Hook Type Menu (6 Types)

### 1. Contrarian Take

**What it does:** names a belief the audience holds (or the industry repeats) and flips it. Creates instant cognitive dissonance — the brain stops to resolve the conflict.

**Template:**
> Everyone says [common belief]. I'm going to tell you why that's keeping you stuck.

**Example:**
> Everyone says consistency is the secret to growth. I'm going to tell you why that advice is keeping most creators invisible.

**Best used when:** you have a genuine counter-position rooted in experience — not just shock value. One contrarian per piece. Do not stack.

---

### 2. Open Loop

**What it does:** opens a question or tension the audience cannot resolve without reading further. The brain hates open loops — it will keep reading to close them.

**Template:**
> [Specific result or moment]. Here's the thing I almost didn't share about how it happened.

**Example:**
> I posted one video and it brought in 40 coaching inquiries in 72 hours. Here's the thing I almost didn't share about how it happened.

**Best used when:** you have a real story with a surprising mechanism. The loop must actually close by the end of the post — never bait-and-switch.

---

### 3. Callout

**What it does:** names the exact person this is for. Creates instant self-identification. The reader feels seen before you say anything else.

**Template:**
> This is for the [specific identity] who [specific situation they are in].

**Example:**
> This is for the healer who has a gift the world needs and has been afraid to charge what it is worth.

**Best used when:** you know your audience's self-image precisely. The callout must be specific enough to exclude people — if it describes everyone, it lands for no one.

---

### 4. Result-Reveal

**What it does:** leads with the outcome, then unpacks how. Stakes the claim in the first line so the audience knows the payoff before committing to read.

**Template:**
> [Specific result] in [specific timeframe / context]. Here is exactly what I did.

**Example:**
> Went from 200 to 4,000 followers in 60 days without running ads. Here is exactly what I did — and what I would skip.

**Best used when:** the result is specific and credible. Vague results ("changed my life") do not count. Include the honest caveat — it adds credibility, not weakness.

---

### 5. Mistake / Confession

**What it does:** leads with vulnerability — something the poster got wrong. Disarms the audience's defenses. Relatable over polished.

**Template:**
> I spent [time/money/effort] doing [thing]. It was a mistake. Here is what I learned.

**Example:**
> I spent two years making content every single day. It burned me out and my engagement dropped. Here is what I learned about sustainable rhythm.

**Best used when:** the mistake is real and the lesson is specific. Manufactured vulnerability reads false. The post must land on a concrete insight, not just empathy.

---

### 6. First-3-Words Rule

**What it does:** the first three words of the hook carry disproportionate weight — they determine whether the eye keeps moving or jumps away. This is a craft rule, not a standalone hook type. Apply it inside any of the 5 types above.

**Rule:**
> Never waste the first three words on setup. The audience, the problem, or the pattern interrupt must appear in words 1–3.

**Strong openings (first 3 words do the work):**
- "Everyone says consistency…" (contrarian)
- "This is for…" (callout)
- "I spent two…" (confession)

**Weak openings (first 3 words are dead weight):**
- "So I wanted to share…" (bury this)
- "Hey guys, today…" (never)
- "I've been thinking…" (setup, no hook)

**Rule of thumb:** if you could delete the first sentence without losing any information, delete it and start with the second.

## Format-Specific First-Beat Guidance

The hook's job differs by format. Each format has one non-negotiable first-beat rule.

| Format | First-Beat Rule | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Reel / short-form video** | Spoken or visual hook must land in **frame 1** — the first second of video. No intro, no logo, no countdown. | The caption hook reinforces but does not substitute. If the first frame is a talking head, the first spoken word must carry the hook. If it is b-roll, the visual must create curiosity before any text appears. |
| **Carousel** | The **cover slide** makes a single promise the next slide must keep. One idea, maximum 10 words. | Slide 2 is the proof or expansion. Never put the hook on slide 2 — the cover is the scroll-stop or there is no scroll. |
| **Static image / single photo** | The **first line of the caption** is the hook — it must stop the thumb before the "more" truncation. Instagram and Facebook cut captions at approximately 125 characters. The hook must live inside that window. | The image supports the hook, not the other way around. If the image alone does not arrest attention, the first caption line must compensate. |
| **Story** | Open with a **poll or question** — make the viewer do something in the first 3 seconds. Passive stories are skipped. | The question must be easy to answer (yes/no, A/B) and create self-identification ("are you doing this too?"). Swipe-up / link tap is the CTA, not a vague "check my bio." |

## Format Variety Rule (H7)

The 5 hero posts (one per content pillar) **must span at least 3 distinct formats.** No all-Reels collapse. No all-carousels default.

When multiple platforms were picked in Phase 0, each hero post names:
1. **Target platform** (Instagram / LinkedIn / TikTok / YouTube Shorts, etc.)
2. **Format** (Reel / carousel / static / story)
3. **Named hook type** (from the 6-type menu above)

**Tag format in `scroll-posts-template.md`:**
```
Platform: [platform]
Format: [format]
Hook type: [hook name]
Thumb-stop check: PASS / FAIL (rewrite if FAIL)
```
