# Hook Library — Headline Creator

## Purpose

Loaded during Phase 3 (Headline Generation). Provides proven headline patterns, winning angle categories, and examples for each tier. Use these as structural inspiration — never copy verbatim. Adapt every pattern to the user's ICA data.

## Preferred Headline Patterns

These patterns tested well across Facebook ads and short-form video hooks. Prefer these structures when generating headlines:

### Pattern 1: Stop/Start Flip
**Structure:** "Stop doing X. Do Y instead."
**Why it works:** Pattern interrupt + immediate reframe. Reader recognizes their behavior, gets a better alternative.
**Ceiling example:** "Stop optimizing your funnel. Optimize your offer instead."
**Floor example:** "Stop posting every day and hoping. Post once with this framework."

### Pattern 2: Conditional Entry
**Structure:** "If you want Z, start with this."
**Why it works:** Self-selection — only the right audience reads past the "if." Creates curiosity about "this."
**Ceiling example:** "If you want clients who pay without negotiating, start with this conversation."
**Floor example:** "If you want your first 10 clients this month, start with this one page."

### Pattern 3: Fastest Path
**Structure:** "The fastest way to go from A to B (without C)."
**Why it works:** Specificity + objection removal. Names the before/after AND removes the biggest fear.
**Ceiling example:** "The fastest way to go from $10k months to $50k months (without hiring a team)."
**Floor example:** "The fastest way to go from idea to paying client (without building a website)."

### Pattern 4: Burden Swap
**Structure:** "You don't need [burden]. You need [lever]."
**Why it works:** Removes perceived prerequisite, replaces with simpler alternative. Relief + curiosity.
**Ceiling example:** "You don't need more leads. You need a better offer."
**Floor example:** "You don't need a business plan. You need your first conversation."

### Pattern 5: Identity Question
**Structure:** "What if [identity] was actually about [unexpected reframe]?"
**Why it works:** Challenges an assumption the reader holds about themselves. Opens a belief gap.
**Ceiling example:** "What if scaling wasn't about more — but about less?"
**Floor example:** "What if getting clients wasn't about marketing at all?"

### Pattern 6: Proof Reversal
**Structure:** "[Common belief] is wrong. Here's what [specific group] actually does."
**Why it works:** Contrarian hook + social proof from aspirational group.
**Ceiling example:** "Posting daily content doesn't build authority. Here's what $100k coaches actually do."
**Floor example:** "You don't need a big following to get clients. Here's what new coaches with full practices actually do."

### Pattern 7: Naming the Exhaustion (Floor-specific)
**Structure:** "Tired of X? There's a simpler path."
**Why it works:** Validates the Floor client's fatigue, then offers relief without promising a fix. Meets them where they are.
**Floor example:** "Tired of posting content nobody sees? There's a simpler path."
**Floor example:** "Tired of second-guessing every offer? There's a clearer way."

### Pattern 8: Permission Grant (Floor-specific)
**Structure:** "You don't have to [burden they carry alone]. [Simpler alternative]."
**Why it works:** Removes the shame of needing help. Floor clients often feel they *should* be able to figure it out. This gives them permission to accept support.
**Floor example:** "You don't have to figure this out alone. One conversation changes everything."
**Floor example:** "You don't have to be an expert to start. You just need the right first step."

### Pattern 9: Objection Dissolve (Floor-specific)
**Structure:** "Everyone says you need X. You don't. Here's why."
**Why it works:** Directly attacks the Floor client's biggest perceived barrier. Removes the prerequisite they think is blocking them.
**Floor example:** "Everyone says you need a big audience first. You don't. Here's why."
**Floor example:** "Everyone says you need to master funnels. You don't. Here's what actually works."

## Brunson Curiosity Formulas (PROVEN — prefer for ad / paid-social hooks)

These three curiosity scripts test consistently highest as paid-ad hooks. Validated example-artist 2026-06-14 (counsel 8.5-9.0). Lead ad creative with these. See `gold-standard-headlines.md` for the full worked set.

### Pattern 10: How-To Without
**Structure:** "How to [achieve desire] without [biggest single objection]."
**Why it works:** Names the exact outcome AND removes the #1 fear in one line. Self-selecting.
**Discipline:** Keep "without" to ONE sharp objection, not two stacked (stacking dilutes).
**Ceiling example:** "How to write your first song without one ounce of natural talent."
**Floor example:** "How to finish your first song without waiting until you feel ready."

### Pattern 11: The Secret / Hack To
**Structure:** "The secret to [result] in [timeframe] (it's not what you'd guess)." / "The hack to finally [result] without [pain]."
**Why it works:** Promises a mechanism + opens a loop. The parenthetical keeps the secret a secret so it pulls the click.
**Discipline:** A secret the reader can already see isn't a secret — keep the loop OPEN. Don't stack two loops (timeframe + secret) without the open-loop tag.
**Ceiling example:** "The secret to writing your first song in one hour (it's not what you'd guess)."
**Floor example:** "The hack to begin your first song without proving you're 'good enough' first."

### Pattern 12: The Reason… Isn't What You Think
**Structure:** "The reason you still haven't [done X] isn't what you think." / "The reason [problem] isn't [blamed surface]. It's [deeper]."
**Why it works:** The #1 open-loop scroll-stopper. Redirects blame from a surface object to an unseen mechanism (the belief-shift engine).
**Discipline:** Powerful once. Max ~1 per set as the hero hook — three of these in a set reads as a trick, not a hand reaching out.
**Ceiling example:** "The reason your first song isn't written... isn't what you think."
**Floor example:** "The reason it feels too late to start isn't true. Here's why."

## Repellent Headline Patterns (Below Floor)

Polite filters that attract the right people by explicitly naming who this is NOT for:

- "Not for people who want a quick fix."
- "Not for anyone who refuses to practice."
- "Not a magic button — bring consistency."
- "If you're looking for overnight results, this isn't for you."
- "Skip this if you want someone to do it all for you."
- "This won't work if you're not willing to show up."

## Angle Categories

When generating headlines, vary across these angle categories. Don't cluster all 10 headlines in one category:

| Angle | Description | Best For |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| Pain Interrupt | Names the problem they're tired of | Floor clients |
| Desire Activation | Paints the outcome they secretly want | Ceiling clients |
| Identity Shift | Reframes who they see themselves as | Ceiling clients |
| Objection Removal | Pre-handles the "but what about..." | Floor clients |
| Curiosity Gap | Opens a loop they need to close | Both tiers |
| Social Proof | References what a specific group does | Both tiers |
| Contrarian | Challenges a common belief | Ceiling clients |
| Relief | Removes a perceived burden | Floor clients |

## Constraint Reminders

- Maximum 14 words per headline (EXCEPT Brunson Curiosity Formulas, Patterns 10-12, which may run to 16 when the "without"/open-loop clause needs it — tightness still wins)
- Verbs over adjectives — every headline should have a strong verb
- No medical, financial, or legal claims
- No hard CTAs (no "Buy now," "Sign up," "Limited time")
- No over-promising timeframes ("in 7 days," "overnight")
- Plain language — no jargon unless the ICA data specifies expert audience
- Use the ICA's own language_swipes when possible — their words, not yours
