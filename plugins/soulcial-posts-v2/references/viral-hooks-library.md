# Viral Hooks Library (v2 superset)

> **Lane note:** The rapid lane uses this full library. soulcial's `hook-bank.md` is the curated 6-type floor + thumb-stop veto — this is the superset it draws from.

A post lives or dies in its first 5 seconds. That opening line is the whole decision: keep reading, or keep scrolling. On short-form video, a clip that keeps half its viewers past the 6-second mark has a real chance to break out. Everything rides on the hook, so it gets written before anything else and reworked until it deserves the next line.

What's here: **100 proven hook frameworks across 13 categories, ordered by virality ceiling** (Receipt, Contrarian, Negative Frame, Stolen Lessons, Curiosity Gap first). Whenever someone asks for a hook, or another skill pulls from this file, match a pattern to the topic, swap in real specifics, draft 3 versions, put each through the first-3-words test, and hand back the winner.

## How to use it (5 steps)

1. **Get topic + angle.** The topic is the subject. The angle is the edge: a strong opinion, a hard number, a client story, a common mistake, or a result someone actually earned. The angle decides the category. When a voice profile or brand context has a **Strong Opinion / Wedge**, feed it into the top-ceiling categories (Contrarian, Negative Frame). No topic? Ask. Don't make one up.
2. **Pick a category** from the angle→category map below. On the fence? Go with **Receipt**, **Contrarian**, or **Stolen Lessons**. They reach furthest.
3. **Fill 3 variations.** Choose 3 templates, all from one category or split across two. Every `[bracket]` gets a real number, name, or moment. A vague fill makes a dead hook.
4. **Run the first-3-words test** (below).
5. **Return the best one**, tell them which category it came from, and list the other 2 as backups. When another skill made the call, give it all 3.

## Angle → Category map

| Angle | Best category |
|---|---|
| An experiment, a result, or a hard number you earned | The Receipt |
| A conviction or wedge most people won't say | Contrarian / Myth-Buster |
| Something people keep getting wrong | Negative Frame |
| A tactic someone else proved first | Stolen Lessons |
| A question you raise and don't answer yet | Curiosity Gap |
| Several points that teach one skill | Listicle / Number |
| Knowledge from inside the room | Secret / Insider |
| One specific crowd you're talking to | Audience Callout |
| A question the reader can't help answering | Question Hook |
| A story, or a change you lived through | Transformation / Story |
| A quick win that takes almost no effort | Speed / Effortless |
| A real reason to move today | Urgency / Stop-Scroll |
| An honest admission about yourself | Confession / Vulnerable |

## The first-3-words test

Cover everything after word 3. Does what's left spark curiosity, surprise, or some feeling?

- "So today I" → **fail** (warm-up, nothing to grab)
- "12 ratios tested" → **pass** (a number, and you want the rest)
- "Everyone's wrong about" → **pass** (picks a fight)
- "7 in 10" → **pass** (a stat that points at the reader)

Trim the warm-up. Put your heaviest word up front.

## The 100-Hook Library

Every template marks the parts you fill with `[brackets]`. Categories run from highest virality ceiling to lowest. Each category carries a generic EXAMPLE. The template itself stays intact.

### 1. The Receipt (proof, numbers, results) — highest ceiling

Proof you earned, a number you can point to, and a loop left open. The reader has to find out how it turned out.

1. [N] [things] tried. [Smaller N] survived.
2. [Time period] of [doing specific thing]. The results surprised me.
3. [Big accomplishment]. [N] lessons I paid for.
4. I went through [N] [things] line by line. The same [N] fixes kept showing up.
5. [N] [experts] got the same question about [topic]. Their answers split.
6. [Past situation] to [result] in [time]. Every step, laid out.
7. [Result] in [time frame]. The exact playbook.
8. Everyone said [claim everyone doubts] couldn't happen. The numbers disagree.
9. [Result] took me [time]. Next up is [goal], and I'm building it in the open.

> Generic: "12 cold-brew ratios tested. Two made the menu."

### 2. Contrarian / Myth-Buster — highest ceiling, polarizing

The reader has to choose a side. Taking a side means commenting, and comments buy reach. Take the wedge straight from the active voice profile.

10. [Common belief]? Almost everyone has it backwards.
11. Quit [common practice]. It stopped working for [outcome] a while ago.
12. Most of what you learned about [subject] doesn't hold up.
13. [Common advice] sounds smart. It falls apart for one reason.
14. [Topic] gets far more credit than it has earned.
15. Hot take: [contrarian take].
16. I don't buy [common belief], and I'll tell you why.
17. Drop what you've been told about [topic]. Start over here.
18. [Popular product] is not worth what people pay for it.

> Generic: "Most roasters think the beans are the product. The product is the first quiet 10 minutes of the day."

### 3. Negative Frame / Mistake Callout

Fear of loss moves people. Dodging a mistake gets the click faster than chasing a win.

19. [N] ways you're quietly sabotaging your [task].
20. Quit [common action] today.
21. Before you [common action], learn these [N] things first.
22. The real reason your [task] keeps falling flat.
23. [N] traps that catch almost everyone doing [task].
24. Hold off on buying [product] until you see this.
25. [Niche] folks, this one habit is costing you.
26. You've had [task] backwards this whole time.
27. I blew it with [thing]. Learn from my mess.

> Generic: "Quit storing your coffee in the freezer. It's flattening the flavor."

### 4. Stolen Lessons / Steal This

A name the reader already trusts, plus a move they can adapt for themselves. Somebody else already proved it works. (Life Gamer Code note: the "steal" here is the platform-native pattern name, not the ethic — frame as *study / model / adapt*.)

28. [Famous person] clears [money] a month. Let's break down how.
29. I modeled [specific thing] for [time]. The result caught me off guard.
30. I ran [famous brand]'s [specific thing] play on my own [business]. [Outcome].
31. A quiet [industry] move most [experts] walk right past.
32. [Famous person] walked away from [business] to bet everything on [venture]. What they saw coming.
33. What [celebrity/influencer] leaves out when they talk about [topic].

> Generic: "I tried a big chain's free-sample playbook at my market stall. Sales doubled that Saturday."

### 5. Curiosity Gap / Open Loop

Raise a question up top and sit on the answer. They keep reading to get it.

34. [N] things about [topic] I'm half surprised I'm allowed to share.
35. The part of [topic] nobody warns you about.
36. Somebody should have told me this years ago.
37. What's really going on behind [situation].
38. What I'd tell myself on day one.
39. I learned this one the expensive way.
40. I tried [action]. I did not see what came next coming.
41. Why is nobody talking about this?
42. A [topic] trick that almost never gets a mention.

> Generic: "The part of running a coffee cart nobody warns you about."

### 6. Listicle / Number Hook

A clear count tells the reader exactly what they'll get and how fast they can skim it. Small and odd numbers usually win.

43. [N] [niche] lessons I learned too late.
44. [N] [niche] tools that gave me back hours every week.
45. [N] [topic] facts that change how you [outcome].
46. [N] signs it's time to [action].
47. [N] upgrades for your [area].
48. [N] steps to get good at [skill] so you can [benefit].
49. [N] quick wins for [outcome].
50. [N] habits to drop this week.

> Generic: "5 roasting lessons I learned too late."

### 7. Secret / Insider

Offers a look behind a closed door. Only use it if you really have been in the room.

51. [Industry] would rather you never figured this out.
52. What actually gets you [outcome] (it's not what they sell you).
53. Something I've never said publicly.
54. The [thing] behind my [result], out in the open.
55. What [experts] keep to themselves.
56. Everyone chases the obvious part of [topic]. The real lever is [secret most people skip].

> Generic: "What actually makes a cup taste like the café (it isn't the machine)."

### 8. Audience Callout / Pattern Interrupt

Point at one exact reader. The person it fits feels seen and stops.

57. [Specific group], this one's for you.
58. If you're a [group], pause here.
59. What [target audience] won't say out loud.
60. Every [group] out there: [promise].
61. Most [audience] miss this completely.
62. [N] out of 10 [niche] get this wrong. You might be one.
63. [Specific group], skip [action]. Let me explain.

> Generic: "Home baristas, this one's for you."

### 9. Question Hook

Ask something the reader answers silently. Once they've answered, they're invested enough to stay.

64. Would you believe [shocking statistic]?
65. Still [common action]?
66. Why does [pain point] keep coming back?
67. Sick of [frustration]? Try this instead.
68. When's the last time you felt [specific emotion]?
69. What if [achieving something] came down to one thing?
70. Want to [goal]? Let me walk you through it.
71. Could you be making this [task] mistake right now?

> Generic: "Still grinding your beans the night before?"

### 10. Transformation / Before-After / Story

Something changed over time. The distance between then and now pulls them in.

72. [Project]: then vs. now.
73. You won't recognize my [area] by the end of this.
74. [Result], and I never needed [common requirement].
75. The failure that ended up paying for everything.
76. I gave myself [hard thing] as a challenge. It didn't go to plan.
77. One week of [thing]. What changed.
78. One small habit rebuilt my [life / health / work].
79. What a day really looks like for a [profession] earning [money] a month.

> Generic: "From a folding table to 3 wholesale accounts, without a single paid ad."

### 11. Speed / Effortless How-To

Tiny effort, quick payoff. Nothing left to scroll away from.

80. [Goal] in [short time]. That's all it takes.
81. [Goal], minus [common obstacle].
82. [Goal] when you're starting from zero.
83. Do [task] the way a [pro] does it.
84. The [time frame] fix for [task].
85. [Goal] without spending much.
86. How I got [desired item] without paying a cent.
87. The fastest path I know to [goal].
88. [Achieve result] in half the time it used to take.

> Generic: "Dial in a new bag of beans in 4 minutes."

### 12. Urgency / FOMO / Stop-Scroll

Give them a reason to move today. Keep it rare, or your audience learns to tune it out.

89. Keep watching if you want to [achieve something].
90. If [benefit] matters to you, read this before you move on.
91. This should be common knowledge.
92. Consider this your nudge to try [activity].
93. [Opportunity] closes soon. Don't sleep on it.
94. After this, you won't look at [topic] the same way.
95. Bookmark this for [the moment you'll need it].

> Generic: "Keep watching if you want your pour-over to taste like the shop's."

### 13. Confession / Vulnerable

Put the persona down. An honest admission builds trust and can pull 3-10x your usual engagement.

96. I've never admitted this, but [admission].
97. You might not like this: [hard truth].
98. Something [audience] really doesn't want to hear.
99. I've been sitting on this one for a while.
100. For years I [did thing wrong]. These days it's [the fix], every time.

> Generic: "I nearly sold the roaster last winter. One customer email stopped me."

## Hook-Writing Rules

Every hook goes through these before it leaves:

- **Specific wins.** "I tested 47 subject lines" hits harder than "I tested a bunch of subject lines." Real numbers, real names, real moments in every placeholder.
- **Lead with the heavy word.** Open on the number, the name, the twist. Cut the throat-clearing: "okay so," "hey guys," "today I want to talk about."
- **Would it work as a post by itself?** If the hook needs the body to make sense, it isn't strong enough yet.
- **Strip the padding.** No "really," "very," "just," "actually," "basically."
- **Skip the stock AI openers.** Never "Picture this," "Are you ready to," "Let me tell you," or "In today's fast-paced world."
- **One idea per hook.** Two hooks stacked together cancel out. Keep the stronger one.
- **Voice-profile precedence.** If the active voice profile documents a hook tic as a protected tic (e.g. a signature metaphor opener, a signature phrase, a 3-dot open loop), that tic is allowed here even if a generic ban list would flag it. The profile wins.

## What NOT to Do

- Never hand back a hook with a `[bracket]` still in it. Every placeholder gets a real specific.
- Never paste the whole library at the user. Give 1 strong hook and 2 backups.
- When the topic carries a strong opinion or a real number, don't settle for a low-ceiling category (Listicle, Question). Those angles belong in Contrarian or Receipt.
- Across a batch, rotate categories. A feed where every post opens the same way starts to look like a template.
- Don't force a signature metaphor where it doesn't fit. A branded variant that feels bolted-on is worse than a clean generic fill. One earned metaphor beats three decorative ones.
