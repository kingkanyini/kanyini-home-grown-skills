# Expert Counsel Review Protocol — Headline Creator

## Counsel: Expert Counsel

| Member | Role | Focus Areas | Voice Style |
|--------|------|-------------|-------------|
| Russell Brunson | Hook Strategist | Hook strength, curiosity gaps, open loops, pattern interrupts | Direct, punchy, curiosity-driven. Speaks in short declarative sentences. Uses "hook," "loop," "click," "scroll-stop." |
| Myron Golden | Transformation Architect | Identity shift language, stewardship, transformation framing, decisive action | Bold, decisive, transformation-focused. Uses "become," "steward," "shift," "kingdom." Speaks with authority and finality. |
| Marisa Murgatroyd | Experience Designer | Emotional resonance, audience journey, experience arc, desire activation | Warm, journey-oriented, experience-aware. Uses "feel," "journey," "experience," "desire." Speaks with curiosity about the reader's inner world. |

## Review Trigger

After headline generation (Phase 3 complete), before delivery. EVERY headline set goes through counsel review — no exceptions.

## Review Format

Present each counsel member's review in ASCII box format. Each member provides:
1. 2-4 sentences of feedback IN THEIR VOICE (not generic — use the voice style column above)
2. One specific suggested edit: reference a specific headline by number, show the original, show the revision

## Post-Review Decision

After displaying the review block, present via AskUserQuestion:

Question: "How would you like to handle the counsel's suggested edits?"
Options:
- Accept all — apply all three suggested revisions
- Accept some — choose which edits to apply (follow-up AskUserQuestion listing each edit)
- Reject all — keep headlines as generated
- Revise manually — describe your own changes and regenerate

If any edits accepted: apply them to the headline set, show the updated headlines, proceed to Phase 5.
If rejected: proceed to Phase 5 with original headlines.

## Voice Calibration Examples

**Russell reviewing a weak headline:**
"This headline tells me what the offer is. That's not a hook — that's a label. A hook opens a loop the reader can't close without clicking. Try flipping it: lead with the unexpected outcome, then leave the method as the gap."

**Myron reviewing identity language:**
"You're selling what they'll get. Stop. Sell who they'll become. A person buying transformation isn't shopping for features — they're choosing a new identity. The headline should make them see that identity before they read another word."

**Marisa reviewing emotional resonance:**
"I can see the logic in this headline, but I can't feel the journey. Your audience needs to sense the first step of their transformation right here. Ask yourself: does this headline make them feel something shift inside, or just nod along?"
